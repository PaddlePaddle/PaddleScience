## 1. Background Introduction

Global medium-range weather forecasting is often an important basis for decision-making in social and economic fields. Traditional numerical weather prediction models generally need to increase computing resources to improve forecast accuracy, and cannot directly use historical weather data to improve the prediction accuracy of the base model. Machine learning-based weather forecasting models can directly use historical data to train models, improve accuracy, and optimize computing resources. At the same time, this data-driven approach allows the model to learn quantitative relationships from data that are not easily expressed by explicit equations, thereby improving prediction accuracy.

GraphCast is a machine learning-based weather forecasting model that can be trained directly from reanalysis data and is capable of predicting hundreds of weather variables globally over 10 days at 0.25° resolution in under a minute. The paper shows that GraphCast significantly outperforms the most accurate operational deterministic systems on 90% of 1380 verification targets, and the model predicts severe weather events well, including tropical cyclones, atmospheric rivers, and extreme temperatures.

## 2. Model Principle

$X^t$ represents the weather state prediction at time t,

$$ X^{t+1}=GraphCast(X^{t}, X^{t-1}) $$

GraphCast generates a prediction sequence of arbitrary length T through autoregressive iteration.

$$ X^{t+1:t+T}=(GraphCast(X^{t}, X^{t-1}), GraphCast(X^{t+1}, X^{t}), ... , GraphCast(X^{t+T-1}, X^{t+T-2}))$$

### 2.1 Model Structure

The core architecture of GraphCast adopts an "Encode-Process-Decode" structure based on Graph Neural Networks (GNN). GNN-based learning simulators are very effective in learning complex physical dynamics of fluids and other materials because their representation and computational structure are similar to learned finite element solvers.

Since the density of the latitude-longitude grid is uneven, GraphCast does not use a latitude-longitude grid internally, but uses a "multi-mesh" representation. The "multi-mesh" is constructed by iteratively refining an icosahedron 6 times, as shown in the figure below, each iteration divides the triangular faces on the polyhedron into 4 smaller faces.

The GraphCast model runs on the graph $\mathcal{G(V^\mathrm{G}, V^\mathrm{M}, E^\mathrm{M}, E^\mathrm{G2M}, E^\mathrm{M2G})}$.

$\mathcal{V^\mathrm{G}}$ is the set of grid points, each grid node represents the vertical slice of the atmosphere at the corresponding latitude and longitude point, and the features of node $v_𝑖^\mathrm{G}$ are represented by $\mathbf{v}_𝑖^\mathrm{G,features}$.

$V^\mathrm{M}$ is the set of mesh nodes, mesh nodes are generated by iterative division of the icosahedron, and the features of node $v_𝑖^\mathrm{M}$ are represented by $\mathbf{v}_𝑖^\mathrm{M,features}$.

$\mathcal{E^\mathrm{M}}$ is a set of undirected edges, where each edge connects a sending mesh node and a receiving mesh node, represented by $e^\mathrm{M}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r}$, and the corresponding features are represented by $\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{M,features}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r}$.

$\mathcal{E^\mathrm{G2M}}$ is a set of undirected edges, where each edge connects a sending grid node and a receiving mesh node, represented by $e^\mathrm{G2M}_{v^\mathrm{G}_s \rightarrow v^M_r}$, and the corresponding features are represented by $\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{G2M,features}_{v^\mathrm{G}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r}$.

$\mathcal{E^\mathrm{M2G}}$ is a set of undirected edges, where each edge connects a sending mesh node and a receiving grid node, represented by $e^\mathrm{M2G}_{v^M_s \rightarrow v^G_r}$, and the corresponding features are represented by $\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{M2G,features}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{G}_r}$.

### 2.2 Encoder

The function of the encoder is to convert data into GraphCast's internal data representation. First, five multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) are used to embed the features of the above five sets into the internal space.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{G}_i = \mathbf{MLP}^\mathrm{embedder}_\mathcal{V^\mathrm{G}}(\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{G,features}_i) \\
\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_i = \mathbf{MLP}^\mathrm{embedder}_\mathcal{V^\mathrm{M}}(\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M,features}_i) \\
\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{M}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} = \mathbf{MLP}^\mathrm{embedder}_\mathcal{E^\mathrm{M}}(\mathbf{e}^{\mathrm{M,features}}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r}) \\
\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{G2M}_{v^\mathrm{G}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} = \mathbf{MLP}^\mathrm{embedder}_\mathcal{E^\mathrm{G2M}}(\mathbf{e}^{\mathrm{G2M,features}}_{v^\mathrm{G}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r}) \\
\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{M2G}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{G}_r} = \mathbf{MLP}^\mathrm{embedder}_\mathcal{E^\mathrm{M2G}}(\mathbf{e}^{\mathrm{M2G,features}}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{G}_r}) \\
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, information is passed from grid nodes to mesh nodes through a Grid2Mesh GNN layer. Edges in $\mathcal{E^\mathrm{G2M}}$ update information through associated nodes.

$$
\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{G2M}_{v^\mathrm{G}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} {'} = \mathbf{MLP}^\mathrm{Grid2Mesh}_\mathcal{E^\mathrm{G2M}}([\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{G2M}_{v^\mathrm{G}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r}, \mathbf{v}_r^\mathrm{G}, \mathbf{v}_s^\mathrm{M}])
$$

Mesh nodes update information through their associated edges.

$$
\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_i {'} = \mathbf{MLP}^\mathrm{Grid2Mesh}_\mathcal{V^\mathrm{M}}([\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_i, \sum_{\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{G2M}_{v^\mathrm{G}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} : v^\mathrm{M}_r=v^\mathrm{M}_i} \mathbf{e}^\mathrm{G2M}_{v^\mathrm{G}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} {'}])
$$

Grid nodes also update information.

$$
\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{G}_i {'} = \mathbf{MLP}^\mathrm{Grid2Mesh}_\mathcal{V^\mathrm{G}}(\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{G}_i)
$$

Finally, three elements are updated through residual connections.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{G}_i \leftarrow \mathbf{v}^\mathrm{G}_i + \mathbf{v}^\mathrm{G}_i {'} \\
\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_i \leftarrow \mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_i + \mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_i {'} \\
\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{G2M}_{v^\mathrm{G}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} = \mathbf{e}^\mathrm{G2M}_{v^\mathrm{G}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} + \mathbf{e}^\mathrm{G2M}_{v^\mathrm{G}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} {'}
\end{aligned}
$$

### 2.3 Processor

The processor contains a Multi-mesh GNN layer, and edges in $\mathcal{E^\mathrm{M}}$ update information through associated nodes.

$$
\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{M}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} {'} = \mathbf{MLP}^\mathrm{Mesh}_\mathcal{E^\mathrm{M}}([\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{M}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r}, \mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_s, \mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_r])
$$

Mesh nodes update information through their associated edges.

$$
\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_i {'} = \mathbf{MLP}^\mathrm{Mesh}_\mathcal{V^\mathrm{M}}([\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_i, \sum_{\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{G2M}_{v^\mathrm{G}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} : v^\mathrm{M}_r=v^\mathrm{M}_i} \mathbf{e}^\mathrm{M}_{v^\mathrm{G}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} {'}])
$$

Finally, elements are updated through residual connections.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_i \leftarrow \mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_i + \mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_i {'} \\
\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{M}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} \leftarrow \mathbf{e}^\mathrm{M}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} + \mathbf{e}^\mathrm{M}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{M}_r} {'}\\
\end{aligned}
$$

### 2.4 Decoder

The function of the decoder is to retrieve information from the mesh back to the grid and make predictions. The decoder contains a Mesh2Grid GNN layer.

Edges in $\mathcal{E^\mathrm{M2G}}$ update information through associated nodes.

$$
\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{M2G}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{G}_r} {'} = \mathbf{MLP}^\mathrm{Mesh2Grid}_\mathcal{E^\mathrm{M2G}}([\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{M2G}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{G}_r},\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_s, \mathbf{v}^\mathrm{M}_r])
$$

Grid nodes update information through their associated edges.

$$
\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{G}_i {'} = \mathbf{MLP}^\mathrm{Mesh2Grid}_\mathcal{V^\mathrm{G}}([\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{G}_i, \sum_{\mathbf{e}^\mathrm{G2M}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{G}_r} : v^\mathrm{G}_r=v^\mathrm{G}_i} \mathbf{e}^\mathrm{M2G}_{v^\mathrm{M}_s \rightarrow v^\mathrm{G}_r} {'}])
$$

Update grid nodes through residual connections.

$$
\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{G}_i \leftarrow \mathbf{v}^\mathrm{G}_i + \mathbf{v}^\mathrm{G}_i {'}
$$

Then use another MLP to process grid information to obtain predicted values.

$$
\mathbf{\hat{y}}^\mathrm{G}_i= \mathbf{MLP}^\mathrm{Output}_\mathcal{V^\mathrm{G}}(\mathbf{v}^\mathrm{G}_i)
$$

Add the input state $X^{t}$ and the predicted value $\hat{Y}^{t}$ to get the next weather state $\hat{X}^{t+1}$

$$ \hat{X}^{t+1} = GraphCast(X^{t}, X^{t-1}) = X^{t} + \hat{Y}^{t} $$

### 3.1 Dataset Introduction

The dataset uses the [2020 reanalysis archive subset](https://paddle-org.bj.bcebos.com/paddlescience/datasets/graphcast/dataset.zip) of ECMWF's ERA5 dataset. The data period is 1979-2018, the time interval is 6 hours (corresponding to 00z, 06z, 12z and 18z every day), the horizontal resolution is 0.25°, and it contains 37 vertical atmospheric pressure levels.

The model predicts a total of 227 target variables, including 5 surface variables and 6 atmospheric variables at each of the 13 pressure levels.
